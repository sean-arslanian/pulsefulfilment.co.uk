Returns

Returns that don't cost you the customer

The Pulse Fulfilment Team 23 April 2026 4 min read

Returns feel like pure loss — the sale reversed, the parcel back, the margin gone. But how you handle a return decides whether you've lost a sale or lost a customer. Get it right and a return can be the moment someone decides to buy from you again.

A return is a service moment, not a failure

Most shoppers expect to return something occasionally. What they remember isn't that they sent it back — it's how easy you made it. A clear, fast, no-drama return tells a customer you're confident in your product and you trust them. That confidence is what brings them back.

Treat returns as friction to be minimised and they quietly erode loyalty. Treat them as a service to be done well and they become a reason to choose you over a rival with a stingier policy.

Speed is the whole experience

The clock that matters to a customer starts when they drop the parcel off and stops when their refund or exchange lands. Everything in between is your problem, not theirs — and the longer it takes, the more anxious emails you'll field.

Fast turnaround means returns are received, inspected and resolved promptly, with the customer kept informed at each step. A refund processed the day a return arrives does more for loyalty than almost anything you can say in marketing.

Nobody minds returning something. They mind chasing you for the refund.

Get good stock back on the shelf — fast

Here's where returns stop being only a cost. A returned item that's still perfect shouldn't sit in a limbo bin for weeks; it should be inspected, restocked and resold. Every unit recovered to sellable condition is margin you keep instead of write off.

That needs a real process: clear grading rules for what's resaleable, what needs refurbishment and what's genuinely scrap — applied consistently, so good stock flows back into inventory quickly and damaged stock doesn't accidentally ship to the next customer.

Make it clear before they buy

  • Publish a plain-English policy — windows, conditions and who pays for return postage, with no surprises.
  • Keep customers informed — "received", "inspected", "refunded" updates remove the need to chase.
  • Track your return reasons — patterns point at sizing, descriptions or packaging you can fix at the source.
  • Resolve edge cases generously — the cost of being kind on a borderline case is almost always less than the cost of losing the customer.
The reframe

Stop asking "how do we reduce returns?" and start asking "how do we make returns so painless that customers buy with confidence — and how do we get good stock back on sale fast?" The first question shrinks your business. The second grows it.

Returns are part of selling online. Handle them quickly, clearly and with a bit of grace, and they protect both your margin and the relationship at the same time.
