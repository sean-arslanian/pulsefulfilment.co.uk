Selling everywhere? Keep your stock in sync

The Pulse Fulfilment Team 7 May 2026 5 min read

More sales channels means more sales — and more ways for the same unit to be sold twice. The brand that lists on Shopify, Amazon, eBay and TikTok Shop has four shopfronts and one shelf. Keeping that shelf honest across all of them is the difference between scaling and apologising.

Why overselling happens

Picture forty units of a best-seller spread across four channels. A shopper buys the last one on TikTok Shop. If Amazon, eBay and Shopify don't hear about it within seconds, they'll happily keep selling stock that's already gone.

Now you're cancelling orders, eating into seller metrics, and turning a sale into a sorry email. On marketplaces it's worse than a missed sale — Amazon and eBay penalise cancellations, and enough of them put your account standing at risk.

One source of truth, syncing both ways

The fix is structural: a single source of truth for inventory that every channel reads from and writes back to. When an order lands anywhere, available stock drops everywhere — fast enough that the next shopper on another channel sees the right number.

"Both ways" matters. It's not enough to push stock levels out to channels; orders have to flow back in, and incoming stock has to update the count as it's booked into the warehouse. Get that loop tight and overselling simply stops being possible.

Four shopfronts, one shelf. The whole game is keeping the shelf honest in real time.

Stop counting stock by hand

Plenty of brands "sync" inventory with a spreadsheet and a daily ritual. It works right up until a flash sale, a viral TikTok, or a Bank Holiday — exactly when the cost of getting it wrong is highest. Manual reconciliation is slow, error-prone, and it scales straight into burnout.

When stock lives with your fulfilment partner and connects automatically to every channel, the count updates as orders are picked and as new inventory arrives. No one re-keys anything. The spreadsheet retires.

A few practical habits

  • Hold a small safety buffer on fast-movers so a sync lag of seconds can never tip you negative.
  • Bundle and kit carefully — multi-SKU bundles need component-level tracking, or one bundle sale silently strands the parts.
  • Watch your marketplace handling times — accurate stock is half the metric; dispatching on time is the other half.
  • Reconcile incoming stock at receipt, not at month-end, so the count is right the moment goods land.
The test

Sell your last unit on one channel. How long until every other channel shows it as out of stock? If the honest answer is "minutes, if someone remembers to update the sheet," you're one busy afternoon away from overselling.

Sell everywhere — that's the goal. Just make sure all those shopfronts are reading from the same honest shelf.
